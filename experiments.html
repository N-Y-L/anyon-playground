Exchange and winding

A braid is the pattern of particle paths through two-dimensional space and time. For identical Abelian anyons, an exchange acts by a phase factor. A counterclockwise exchange multiplies the state by \(e^{i\theta}\).

Equations, conventions & limits

\[U_{\mathrm{exchange}} = e^{i\theta}\]

\[U_{\mathrm{winding}} = e^{2i\theta}\]

Counterclockwise is positive; clockwise reverses the sign of the angle. The displayed multiplier is the result of a completed braid. The animation gives no physical meaning to a phase along an unfinished path.

This is the statistical contribution for identical Abelian anyons in the plane. Dynamical phases, interactions, and microscopic motion are omitted. The continuous angle slider explores Abelian statistics; it does not claim that every value occurs in one material.

One enclosed semion shifts the fringe by half a cycle. A second restores the original fringe: phase is defined modulo \(2\pi\).

Equations, conventions & limits

\[\delta = 2Nw\theta\]

\[P_0 = \frac{1}{2}\left[1 + V\cos(\phi + \delta)\right]\]

\[P_1 = 1 - P_0\]

\(N\) is the number of enclosed anyons; \(w\) is the signed winding number. The reference phase \(\phi\) represents the controlled phase difference between the paths. A positive statistical phase moves the curve to the left.

The contrast parameter \(V\) summarizes loss of visibility; it is not a microscopic noise model. Real anyon interferometers can also include electromagnetic phases, changing charge, interactions, and device-specific tunneling. A shifted cosine by itself does not establish anyonic statistics.

Equations, conventions & limits

\[\sigma\times\sigma = 1 + \psi\]

\[B_1 = \begin{pmatrix}e^{-i\pi/8} & 0 \\ 0 & e^{3i\pi/8}\end{pmatrix}\]

\[B_2 = F B_1 F,\qquad B_3 = B_1\]

\[F = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix}1 & 1 \\ 1 & -1\end{pmatrix}\]

The two-dimensional space has basis \(\lvert 0\rangle\) (the first pair has vacuum charge \(1\)) and \(\lvert 1\rangle\) (the first pair has topological charge \(\psi\), the fermionic type). The preparation \(\lvert +\rangle\) is a coherent superposition of these basis states, with the total charge of all four anyons still vacuum.

Buttons append operations in chronological order: \(B_1\to B_2\) produces \(B_2B_1\lvert\mathrm{initial}\rangle\). Reversing the order retains each exchange's direction; it does not undo the sequence. To undo a braid, reverse its order and invert each exchange.

The squared overlap is \(\left|\langle\mathrm{state\ A}\vert\mathrm{state\ B}\rangle\right|^2\). Equal probabilities in this one measurement basis need not imply equal states. The model assumes ideal braiding within a fixed fusion space; it does not model state preparation, measurement dynamics, finite separation, or errors.

WHAT TO NOTICE

Adding one anyon reuses the previous counts. The two sequences follow the Fibonacci recurrence, even with the total charge fixed.

Equations, conventions & limits

\[a_{n+1} = b_n,\qquad b_{n+1} = a_n + b_n\]

\[(a_1,b_1) = (0,1)\]

Here \(a_n\) counts sequential fusion paths ending in vacuum, and \(b_n\) those ending in \(\tau\). A vacuum intermediate charge must fuse with the next \(\tau\) to give \(\tau\); a \(\tau\) intermediate charge permits either outcome.

With \(F_0 = 0\) and \(F_1 = 1\), the dimensions are \(a_n = F_{n-1}\) and \(b_n = F_n\) for \(n\geq 1\). Their asymptotic growth per added anyon is the golden ratio \(\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}\), called the quantum dimension of \(\tau\).

This experiment counts ideal fusion-space basis states. The chart is not a probability distribution or a braid simulation. A chosen total charge must be held fixed when interpreting the available state space.

Loops in the toric code

In the toric code, an electric-type excitation \(e\) picks up \(-1\) when it winds once around a magnetic-type excitation \(m\). These are quasiparticles: localized collective excitations of a many-body system.

WHAT TO NOTICE

Move an \(m\) excitation outside the loop and the sign stops depending on it. Two enclosed \(m\) excitations cancel each other's minus signs.

Equations, conventions & limits

\[U_{e\text{ loop}} = (-1)^{N_m w}\]

\(N_m\) counts enclosed \(m\) excitations and \(w\) counts complete windings. The \(e\) and \(m\) types each have bosonic self-exchange statistics, but their mutual winding gives \(-1\). Their composite \(\varepsilon = e\times m\) is a fermion. This sign is measurable only relative to a reference process; by itself it does not change an outcome probability.

This is a local patch and a contractible loop in the ideal toric-code model. The grid is a diagram of excitations and winding, not a simulation of microscopic spins. Toggling a square edits the configuration; it is not a physical single-excitation creation process. Compensating excitations may lie outside the displayed patch. Noncontractible loops, boundaries, thermal dynamics, and error correction are outside this experiment.
