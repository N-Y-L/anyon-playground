Anyons and quantum transport

Neil Yuanting Li

What is an anyon, and how could an experiment identify one? Start with exchanging two identical particles. Then ask what, inside a material, could behave as a particle with a different exchange rule.

Start reading: what makes an anyon? →

Read the connected notes from top to bottom. They assume graduate quantum mechanics and mathematics; the physical meaning of anyons is the subject to be developed. The figures appear beside the calculations they explain.

Reading order

  1. What does exchanging two objects do to their quantum state?
  2. What can move like a particle inside a material?
  3. How can a small region carry a fraction of an electron’s charge?
  4. What happens when two such regions are exchanged?
  5. How do we describe and prepare two of these moving objects?
  6. How does an applied potential move them?
  7. How do we separate the effect of exchange from interference in the apparatus? Then compare with a measured signal.
  8. What remains to be calculated?
  9. What changes when an exchange acts by a matrix?
