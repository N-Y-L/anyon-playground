Anyons: notes and experiments

Neil Yuanting Li

A small collection of calculations, interactive examples, and reading notes. An anyon is a particle-like excitation in two dimensions whose exchange behavior can go beyond the familiar bosonic or fermionic sign. These pages assume elementary quantum mechanics, but no prior knowledge of anyons.

Start with the short notes, or try the first two examples below. All examples run locally in a browser.

Interactive examples

  1. Exchange and winding — compare one exchange with a full encircling.
  2. An ideal interferometer — see how an enclosed anyon shifts an interference pattern.
  3. Order of Ising braids — exchange different pairs in different orders and compare the outcomes.
  4. Fibonacci fusion states — count the states allowed by one simple combination rule.
  5. Loops in the toric code — move one kind of excitation around another and track the resulting sign.

Each example states its equations and assumptions. These are small models of exchange, fusion, and interference; they do not simulate an entire material. Ising anyons, Fibonacci anyons, and toric-code excitations are different models.
