Anyons: visual notes

Neil Yuanting Li

Exchange two identical particles. Everything looks as it did before. Surely nothing happened? Quantum mechanics lets the state remember the journey. In a plane, that memory has more possibilities than the familiar boson or fermion sign. Anyons are two-dimensional excitations with exchange statistics beyond the usual boson and fermion possibilities. These notes begin with that puzzle and follow it into interference, correlations, and quantum information. Nayak et al.'s review gives the larger picture.

The fun is to make a prediction before touching a control. Does exchanging twice undo the exchange? If two measurements agree, must the states agree? Can a string grow longer without costing more energy? Each figure gives you something small enough to calculate, and a reason to look twice. Try a prediction, change a control, then open the explanation to find the mechanism.

Assumed background: graduate quantum mechanics and some condensed matter. We use states, matrices, interference, and spin operators. No quantum field theory or prior anyon knowledge is needed; new concepts are introduced where they become useful.

Seven visual notes

NoteThe puzzleA familiar starting point
1. Exchange and windingThe particles return to their starting positions. Why might the state fail to return?Global phase; inverse operations
2. An ideal interferometerA global phase is invisible. How do we persuade it to show itself?Adding amplitudes; density matrices
3. Ising braidsThe same exchanges in a different order can give different answers. Which measurement catches the difference?Noncommuting unitaries; changing basis
4. Fibonacci fusion pathsWhere do the extra quantum states live if no single anyon carries a little spin?Collective basis states; Hilbert-space dimension
5. The toric codeCan ordinary Pauli operators create particles with an unusual winding phase?Commuting spin Hamiltonians; operator algebra
6. Do anyons bunch?Keep the exchange rule fixed. Can changing the preparation reverse a correlation's sign?Landau levels; expectation values in a prepared state
7. A memory with no local cluesCreate a pair, walk one around, annihilate it. The defects are gone—but what happened to the stored state?Pauli strings; simultaneous eigenstates

Start with 1 and 2. For particle correlations, try 6 next; for quantum information, follow 3, 4, 5, and 7. Example buttons set up a useful comparison; reset lets you start again.

The notes use several different anyon models. Their shared theme is what an exchange or a collective state can do; their particles, Hamiltonians, and preparation rules are specified separately.

Use and modify

The figures and equations run in your browser. To explore offline, download the ZIP from the GitHub repository, extract it, and open index.html. No installation is needed; external reading links need a connection. Try a calculation, reset an example, or save a vector figure and its parameters.
