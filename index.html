Anyons: visual notes

Neil Yuanting Li

Return two identical particles to their starting positions. The arrangement is unchanged, but the quantum state can remember how they got there. In two dimensions, exchange permits more than the boson or fermion sign. The excitations that use those possibilities are called anyons.

These notes ask what that memory does, how to calculate it, and what a measurement would actually reveal. The figures compare things that are easy to confuse: the same final positions with different winding, the same local defects with different stored information, or the same fusion rules with different statistics. Each control changes a specified model; the equation beside it says what is being calculated.

Assumed background: graduate quantum mechanics and some condensed matter. No quantum field theory or prior anyon knowledge. For the physical starting point—quasiparticles, an energy gap, and why electrons can support fractional excitations—read how an anyon arises. The connected notes follow the sequence below; annotated sources lead into the research literature.

A case study: statistics meets preparation

Do anyons bunch? Hold the statistics fixed and change a lowest-Landau-level pair preparation. Its mean squared separation can pass from above to below a distinguishable-particle reference. Follow the angular-state weights to see why; a statistical angle alone does not specify a correlation experiment.

Within each group, follow the listed order on a first pass. These are distinct models: the toric code is a spin Hamiltonian, Fibonacci and Ising examples specify fusion and braid data, and the pair calculation specifies a quantum Hall preparation. The spin in the Berry note teaches a calculation method; it is not itself an anyon. Assumptions, units, conventions, and sources are given where they are used.

Take a calculation further

The inspiration catalog connects these calculations to packet dynamics, experimental interference, quantum gates, and error correction. Implemented models and proposed extensions are identified separately. The research questions collect the checks needed to connect ideal anyon data to a physical system.

Use and modify

Everything runs in your browser, including the equations. To explore offline, download the ZIP from the GitHub repository, extract it, and open index.html. No installation is needed; external sources need a connection. Figures can be saved as SVG, with parameters and results in JSON.
