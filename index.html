Anyons: visual notes

Neil Yuanting Li

In two dimensions, exchanging identical excitations can change a quantum state by a phase or by a matrix acting on a shared state space. These notes explore what those statements mean, how to calculate their consequences, and which consequences a measurement can reveal.

Assumed background: graduate quantum mechanics and some condensed matter. State vectors, unitary matrices, Pauli operators, and density matrices are used explicitly. No quantum field theory or prior knowledge of anyons is assumed; fusion, braiding, and topological charge are introduced along the way.

Start with exchange and interference. Each note places a model, its adjustable figure, and the reasoning behind it together. The example buttons load specific comparisons worth examining.

Five visual notes

NoteQuestion to investigateUseful QM idea
1. Exchange and windingWhy is exchanging twice different from exchanging and undoing? What survives a change of path shape?Global phase; inverse operations
2. An ideal interferometerHow does a braid phase shift a probability? What does losing coherence actually remove?Adding amplitudes; density matrices
3. Ising braidsWhen does changing the order change the state? Can one measurement hide the difference?Noncommuting unitaries; changing basis
4. Fibonacci fusion pathsWhat are the states being counted? Why are the dimensions Fibonacci numbers?Basis vectors; dimension of a Hilbert space
5. The toric codeHow do Pauli operators create pairs of excitations, and why does a loop acquire a minus sign?Commuting spin Hamiltonians; operator algebra

The Ising, Fibonacci, and toric-code examples describe different anyon models. Each calculation states its assumptions. Their purpose is to make particular ideas visible, rather than simulate a complete material or device.

Read the argument in one place

Anyons: paths, states, and measurements connects the five examples, explains what a quasiparticle is, and introduces the role of a gap and adiabatic transport. It includes a brief connection to Laughlin quantum Hall quasiholes. (Markdown with LaTeX source)

Questions to pursue next

The inspiration catalog gives eight directions: pair correlations, squeezing at a saddle, motion in traps, experimental interference, collider geometry, path deformation, Fibonacci gates, and logical memory. Each entry identifies a minimal model, an observable, and a pitfall. The annotated sources provide routes into the papers and useful existing code.

Use and modify

Open this file in a browser; the figures and LaTeX rendering work locally without installation or internet. Use the controls to change a calculation, reset an example, and save its vector figure or parameters.
