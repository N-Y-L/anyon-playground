Anyons and quantum transport

Neil Yuanting Li

I want to understand what a quantum state remembers after I exchange two identical objects—and how an experiment could notice. These are my notes from that first question to a few directions worth exploring.

Read the notes: what makes an anyon?

I assume graduate mathematics, basic graduate quantum mechanics and very basic condensed-matter physics. Definitions accompany the ideas as they appear. Optional derivations give the assumptions and calculations in more detail.

First, the ideas

  1. A particle made from a many-body state. The electrons stay electrons; their excitations can behave differently.
  2. A hole with a fraction of an electron's charge. No electron needs to be cut into pieces.
  3. A state that remembers a winding. Exchange, encircling and ordinary magnetic phases play different roles.
  4. An exchange that rotates a collective state. This is the extra structure of non-Abelian anyons.
  5. A braid in an interference signal. The experiment has to separate statistics from electrostatics.

A closer look at preparation and measurement

I can ask how preparation changes a pair, how a saddle amplifies a correlation, or how an extended collider complicates the reference. A saddle is a potential that rises along one direction and falls along another. An extended collider lets a particle follow several paths before it reaches a detector. The notes introduce these models before working through their equations.

A few papers and an open question

Three papers I want to remember gives me a result, its scope and a question for each: a phase derived, a phase measured, and a noncommuting exchange. An optional fourth explains how a reference measurement changes the interpretation of a collider signal.

The questions to explore and seven directions branch into detectors, saddles, wires, closed surfaces, Möbius geometry and ramps. I can follow an interesting connection without already having a research proposal.
