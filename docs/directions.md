# Directions to think about

In the [anyon notes](notes.md), I followed an excitation from an electron fluid to a possible measurement. I now want to understand what changes when I alter the dynamics, geometry or measurement. The examples below give me several ways to ask that question.

Several directions connect to work by Smitha Vishveshwara and her collaborators. Each begins with a physical question, then uses a small model to examine it. The optional calculations retain the definitions and assumptions behind the figures.

The [single-particle saddle](directions/saddle.html) continues the transport problem. The [sphere](directions/surfaces.html) and [Möbius band](directions/orientation.html) instead ask which quantum states are compatible with a changed surface.

## Seven changes to the problem

### 1. One particle at a saddle

I use a potential with opposite curvatures in two directions. It produces **squeezing**, contraction of one quantum width accompanied by expansion of its conjugate width. The same unstable dynamics connects scattering and decaying signals in several physical systems. I want to know which connections survive changes of state and measurement.

[Single-particle calculation](directions/saddle.html)

### 2. What the detector measures

I want to understand what interference and current fluctuations reveal about anyons. Measuring charge, heat flow or the effect of a braid—an exchange history—constrains different properties of the state.

[Observables and statistical inference](directions/detectors.html)

### 3. End modes in a finite wire

A superconducting wire can support modes localized near its ends, with amplitudes that decay into the interior. I ask how information shared by these modes coexists with a small energy splitting, and what remains protected when the wire is finite or its potential is nonuniform.

[Finite wires and localization](directions/wires.html)

### 4. Phase winding on a closed surface

Around a zero of a complex field, the phase can change by an integer multiple of $2\pi$. That integer is its **winding**. On a sphere, the total winding is constrained by how the field is defined over the whole surface. Magnetic flux changes that constraint.

[Phase winding and magnetic flux](directions/surfaces.html)

### 5. Geometry without a global orientation

A normal followed continuously around a Möbius band returns reversed. I ask how a quantum state with a preferred handedness can fit that surface, while distinguishing the geometric reversal from a spin rotation or an anyon exchange.

[Möbius geometry and compatible models](directions/orientation.html)

### 6. Ramps and conserved parity

A **ramp** changes a Hamiltonian parameter over time. A small energy gap to accessible states makes adiabatic following difficult; a conserved quantity can forbid the desired transition altogether. I use conservation of fermion **parity**, the evenness or oddness of particle number, to separate the two effects.

[Adiabatic following and parity blocking](directions/quenches.html)

### 7. Exchange rules in different settings

I compare particles on a line, loop excitations in three dimensions and non-Abelian anyons. Each changes either the allowed motions or the states on which those motions act. For the non-Abelian case, **fusion channels**—the combined excitation types of pairs—label the collective states.

[Configuration spaces, loops and fusion](directions/statistics.html)

## The comparison behind each direction

In each example I distinguish a constraint imposed by topology or symmetry from the dynamics of a particular state. I then ask which measurement could reveal the distinction. This gives me a common way to discuss the directions without treating them as a list of projects.

The [reading notes and talks](directions/reading.html) give me a small set of starting points; the [source list](directions/sources.html) keeps the wider references available. [Questions to explore](catalog.md) returns to the original two-anyon problem.
