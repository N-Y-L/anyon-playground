Preparation, motion and detection

These calculations examine how preparation and propagation turn an exchange rule into a measured signal. They use the Laughlin quasiholes, Landau levels, exchange statistics and Berry phase introduced in the main notes. The two-particle preparations lead to a saddle model; the final collider calculation uses bosons and fermions to isolate the effect of one-particle interference.

Replace the electron fluid by two effective particles

Two preparations can give different mean squared separations at the same charge and exchange angle. The exchange rule restricts the allowed states; preparation determines their amplitudes.

Vishveshwara and Cooper (2010) retain only pair states in the lowest effective Landau level, a restriction called projection. The guiding center is the center of a cyclotron orbit. Their observable is the mean squared separation of the two guiding centers. They compare it with two distinguishable particles prepared with the same localization labels and packet widths. Distinguishable particles carry labels that can tell them apart, so their exchanged alternatives do not interfere.

The localization label ddd specifies the intended relative separation before projection; it approximates a measured packet separation only for distant packets. At fractional statistics, the difference in mean squared separation from the distinguishable reference can change sign as ddd changes. The exchange angle has remained fixed. Thus a fixed exchange angle does not prescribe the sign of this separation comparison.

The calculation compares this preparation with another family used by Subramanyan and Vishveshwara (2019). These are generalized coherent states, defined below as eigenstates of a specified lowering operator. The families agree for bosons and fermions but give different angular-state weights for fractional statistics. Which family, if either, describes a state released from a particular trap depends on its preparation.

Which localized state?

The alternative preparation used in Subramanyan and Vishveshwara’s dynamics study is defined by operators that preserve the exchange condition. A linear relative coordinate changes angular momentum by one unit and takes a state out of the chosen sector. Let a saddle amplify a correlation

A saddle potential curves upward along one direction and downward along a perpendicular direction. When a splitter has an interior

A beam splitter couples two incoming channels to two outgoing channels; each channel is a propagating mode. I first take a lossless splitter whose scattering amplitudes are effectively constant over the incident packet's wave numbers. A balanced splitter sends one incident particle to either output with probability 1/21/21/2. I inject one particle in each input, with identical synchronized packets and matching internal states. Bosons then leave together; fermions leave in different outputs. The alternatives “both transmit” and “both reflect” interfere. This conclusion depends on the matching of the packets and on the one-particle scattering amplitudes.

Samal, Vishveshwara, Gefen and Väyrynen (2026) studied an extended collider in which a particle can make several trips around an antidot—a depleted region with a circulating edge path—before exiting. One particle already interferes with itself. A comparison that discards this coherence can suggest bunching, an excess of same-output events relative to the chosen reference, even for fermions.

I therefore keep the apparatus's one-particle interference in the reference calculation, while making the two particles distinguishable. Subtracting this reference isolates the exchange contribution in the model. The paper does this for noninteracting bosons and fermions. It leaves an anyonic extension open, because even a single-source process can contain braiding information.

An anyonic extension must specify the contributing paths and tunneling processes. Replacing the bosonic or fermionic exchange sign by eiθe^{i\theta}eiθ alone does not determine the result.
