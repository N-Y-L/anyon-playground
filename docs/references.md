# Sources

Links beside the [calculations](notes.md) identify their scientific starting points. These short routes lead to longer accounts and to the primary work.

## The electron fluid and its excitations

[Tong’s quantum Hall lectures, §§3.1–3.2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.06687) develop Laughlin wavefunctions, the plasma argument and fractional statistics before the later field-theory treatment. [Simon’s lecture notes, Ch. 20](https://www-thphys.physics.ox.ac.uk/people/SteveSimon/topological2019/Topobook-Oct18-2019.pdf) offer another physical account of quasiholes and their transport. Both are useful extended expositions; the conventions in this site are stated in its own calculations.

[Laughlin, 1983](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.50.1395) constructs the fractional Hall state and its charged excitations. [Arovas, Schrieffer and Wilczek, 1984](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.53.722) compute quasihole statistics by Berry transport. [Leinaas and Myrheim, 1977](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02727953) give the configuration-space argument for allowed particle statistics.

[Fulsebakke and collaborators, 2023](https://doi.org/10.21468/SciPostPhys.14.6.149) supply the numerical Laughlin quasihole density profile reproduced in the notes. Figure 11(a) compares finite spherical systems with a thermodynamic extrapolation; the [figure caption](notes.md#figure-charge) gives its source and reuse license.

[Kjønsberg and Leinaas, 1997](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/9606214) is especially useful at the transition to an effective anyon coordinate: it constructs the quasihole map and distinguishes projected-coordinate states from generalized coherent states. Its complex conjugation and operator factors matter when comparing conventions.

## Preparation, dynamics and measurements

[Vishveshwara and Cooper, 2010](https://arxiv.org/abs/0908.3945) — localized-pair weights and the excess mean squared separation. Published in Physical Review B. The site's original correlation notebook reproduces this preparation; the main notes add its comparison with another state family.

[Subramanyan and Vishveshwara, 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.00442) — generalized coherent states, harmonic and saddle motion, and proposed bulk interferometry. Published in Journal of Statistical Mechanics. The state construction is part of the physical model.

[Basani, Subramanyan and Vishveshwara, 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.15488) — quadratic-potential dynamics and its symmetries. An arXiv preprint in the version checked for these notes.

[Samal, Vishveshwara, Gefen and Väyrynen, 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.19674) — self-interference and calibrated two-particle references for extended colliders. Published in Physical Review Letters. Its boson/fermion result leaves an anyonic extension open.

[Nakamura and collaborators, 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.14115) — conductance phase slips at filling 1/3 and the electrostatic checks needed to interpret them as braiding. [Published experiment](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41567-020-1019-1).

## Sources for the independent notebooks

For the seven follow-on reading routes, see [Sources for these directions](directions/sources.md).

- [Berry, 1984](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspa.1984.0023), [Wilczek–Zee, 1984](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.52.2111), and [Fukui–Hatsugai–Suzuki, 2005](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/0503172): geometric transport, its degenerate generalization and gauge-invariant overlap products. The [spin notebook](../berry.html) uses the loop-product method.
- [Kitaev](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/9707021) and [Dennis and collaborators](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0110143): toric-code strings, ground-state information and error correction. The [lattice figures](../experiments.html#toric) implement string algebra, not a full noisy memory.
- [Wen](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/9506066), [Lu–Vishwanath, 2012](https://arxiv.org/abs/1205.3156), and [Lu–Vishwanath, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1302.2634): Abelian K matrices, charge vectors, and toric-code/double-semion examples used in the [Abelian notebook](../abelian.html).
- [Nayak and collaborators](https://arxiv.org/abs/0707.1889), [Ivanov](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/0005069), and [Bonderson–Shtengel–Slingerland](https://arxiv.org/abs/0707.4206): fusion spaces, non-Abelian transport and interferometry. The [Ising figure](../experiments.html#braids) uses the stated Ising matrices, including their common phases.
- [Trebst and collaborators](https://arxiv.org/abs/0902.3275) and [Bseiso and collaborators, §II.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.21761v2#S2.SS1): Fibonacci bases and the numerical exchange convention used in the [fusion notebook](../fusion-basis.html). [Feiguin and collaborators](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/0612341) apply this recoupling to an interacting anyon chain.
- [Bonesteel and collaborators](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0505065) and [Bravyi](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0511178): contrasting computational capabilities of Fibonacci and Ising braiding. [Cheng–Galitski–Das Sarma](https://arxiv.org/abs/1106.2549) analyze limits of adiabatic braiding in a microscopic setting.
