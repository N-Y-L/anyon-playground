Sources

Links beside the calculations identify their scientific starting points. These short routes lead to longer accounts and to the primary work.

The electron fluid and its excitations

Tong’s quantum Hall lectures, §§3.1–3.2 develop Laughlin wavefunctions, the plasma argument and fractional statistics before the later field-theory treatment. Simon’s lecture notes, Ch. 20 offer another physical account of quasiholes and their transport. Both are useful extended expositions; the conventions in this site are stated in its own calculations.

Laughlin, 1983 constructs the fractional Hall state and its charged excitations. Arovas, Schrieffer and Wilczek, 1984 compute quasihole statistics by Berry transport. Leinaas and Myrheim, 1977 give the configuration-space argument for allowed particle statistics.

Fulsebakke and collaborators, 2023 supply the numerical Laughlin quasihole density profile reproduced in the notes. Figure 11(a) compares finite spherical systems with a thermodynamic extrapolation; the figure caption gives its source and reuse license.

Kjønsberg and Leinaas, 1997 is especially useful at the transition to an effective anyon coordinate: it constructs the quasihole map and distinguishes projected-coordinate states from generalized coherent states. Its complex conjugation and operator factors matter when comparing conventions.

Preparation, dynamics and measurements

Vishveshwara and Cooper, 2010 — localized-pair weights and the excess mean squared separation. Published in Physical Review B. The site's original correlation notebook reproduces this preparation; the main notes add its comparison with another state family.

Subramanyan and Vishveshwara, 2019 — generalized coherent states, harmonic and saddle motion, and proposed bulk interferometry. Published in Journal of Statistical Mechanics. The state construction is part of the physical model.

Basani, Subramanyan and Vishveshwara, 2025 — quadratic-potential dynamics and its symmetries. An arXiv preprint in the version checked for these notes.

Samal, Vishveshwara, Gefen and Väyrynen, 2026 — self-interference and calibrated two-particle references for extended colliders. Published in Physical Review Letters. Its boson/fermion result leaves an anyonic extension open.

Nakamura and collaborators, 2020 — conductance phase slips at filling 1/3 and the electrostatic checks needed to interpret them as braiding. Published experiment.

Sources for the independent notebooks

For the seven follow-on reading routes, see Sources for these directions.

  • Berry, 1984, Wilczek–Zee, 1984, and Fukui–Hatsugai–Suzuki, 2005: geometric transport, its degenerate generalization and gauge-invariant overlap products. The spin notebook uses the loop-product method.
  • Kitaev and Dennis and collaborators: toric-code strings, ground-state information and error correction. The lattice figures implement string algebra, not a full noisy memory.
  • Wen, Lu–Vishwanath, 2012, and Lu–Vishwanath, 2016: Abelian K matrices, charge vectors, and toric-code/double-semion examples used in the Abelian notebook.
  • Nayak and collaborators, Ivanov, and Bonderson–Shtengel–Slingerland: fusion spaces, non-Abelian transport and interferometry. The Ising figure uses the stated Ising matrices, including their common phases.
  • Trebst and collaborators and Bseiso and collaborators, §II.1: Fibonacci bases and the numerical exchange convention used in the fusion notebook. Feiguin and collaborators apply this recoupling to an interacting anyon chain.
  • Bonesteel and collaborators and Bravyi: contrasting computational capabilities of Fibonacci and Ising braiding. Cheng–Galitski–Das Sarma analyze limits of adiabatic braiding in a microscopic setting.
