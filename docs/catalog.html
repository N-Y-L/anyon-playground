Questions to explore

After the connected notes, I have a few directions rather than a fixed project. What attracts me is the gap between a clean exchange rule and a real preparation or measurement. Each question below changes one ingredient and asks what survives.

How much does a packet remember its preparation?

The exchange rule determines allowed states, but it does not choose their amplitudes. The two localized-pair preparations already produce different fractional-statistics correlations. I would like to understand which features survive across preparations, and which encode how the excitations were localized or released.

Vishveshwara and Cooper and Subramanyan and Vishveshwara give concrete starting points. Their state families are useful examples, not interchangeable definitions of an anyon. Kjønsberg and Leinaas connects this issue to the microscopic quasihole description.

Which observable tells me what I want to know?

A separation moment averages a power of the distance between particles. A coincidence probability counts joint detector events. A current correlation measures how currents fluctuate together. These observables weight an outgoing state differently. I want to understand what information each makes accessible, and which comparisons remain meaningful when the detector changes.

The saddle makes this question visible. The detector direction broadens it to edge transport. A useful calculation would have to specify its state and measured operator; I do not need to prescribe that calculation before deciding which physical question interests me.

What should a reference preserve?

The extended collider shows why interference between paths available to one particle belongs in a comparison intended to isolate mutual exchange. Anyons make the question richer: some single-source histories can already carry braiding information. How should a reference retain the information I want while removing an unwanted contribution?

Interferometry poses a related problem. A charge transition can change both the statistical phase and the area enclosed by a path. The Nakamura experiment makes the link between topology and electrostatics concrete.

What changes with geometry or a drive?

I can follow seven directions: saddle potentials, detectors, wire end states, closed surfaces, Möbius geometry, changes of Hamiltonian parameters in time, and other exchange settings. These are different models, so I first ask what moves, which paths or states are available, and what is measured. The connection to anyons may be direct, or it may be a shared question about topology and dynamics.

For another microscopic example, the toric-code notebook starts from a spin Hamiltonian and shows how moving one type of excitation around another produces a phase. Its memory extension asks how local defects can disappear while quantum information stored in global properties of the state changes. The fusion-basis notebook explores the collective states that remain when particle types and positions are fixed.

These are broad entry points, not claims that the open questions are new or that a project is already feasible. I can use the paper shortlist to begin a conversation, then choose the direction worth understanding more deeply.

Tools if a question eventually becomes a calculation

QuTiP provides quantum-state dynamics; qecsim and Stim address stabilizer-code and error-correction calculations. They solve different problems. None is needed to read or run this site, and none supplies the missing physical model by itself.
