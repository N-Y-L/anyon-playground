8. Calculate a geometric phase

An eigensolver gives you a state vector, but its overall phase is arbitrary. Run it again at a nearby field direction and the phase may be different. How can these unreliable arrows reveal a reliable Berry phase? Close the loop: every arbitrary phase then has a partner that cancels it.

Start with one spin, not an anyon. Its Hamiltonian is \(H=-(\Delta/2)\boldsymbol n\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma\), with \(\Delta>0\) the energy gap and \(\boldsymbol\sigma\) the Pauli matrices. The ground spin points along the unit vector \(\boldsymbol n\). Turn that direction around a latitude of polar angle \(\beta\):

\[\boldsymbol n=(\sin\beta\cos\phi,\sin\beta\sin\phi,\cos\beta),\qquad |u\rangle=\begin{pmatrix}\cos(\beta/2)\\e^{i\phi}\sin(\beta/2)\end{pmatrix}.\]

Positive motion increases \(\phi\): it is counterclockwise when viewed from \(+z\). For slow transport, the geometric phase is half the negative oriented solid angle, \(\gamma=-\Omega/2\), where \(\Omega=\pm2\pi(1-\cos\beta)\). This is separate from the dynamical phase \(-\int E\,dt/\hbar\). We calculate only the geometric contribution. Berry’s original paper gives the spin result.

Phases differing by \(2\pi\) give the same multiplier. At the south pole the displayed smooth phase is \(\mp2\pi\), equivalent to zero; at the equator either principal value \(+\pi\) or \(-\pi\) represents \(-1\). The error compares the phase factors, not a naive difference of displayed angles.

Watch the arbitrary phases cancel

For normalized neighboring states with nonzero overlap, retain only the overlap’s phase. The last link returns to the first stored state:

\[z_j=\frac{\langle u_{j+1}|u_j\rangle}{|\langle u_{j+1}|u_j\rangle|},\qquad W_N=\prod_{j=0}^{N-1}z_j,\qquad u_N=u_0,\qquad \gamma_N=\arg W_N.\]

A new convention \(|u_j\rangle\mapsto e^{i\alpha_j}|u_j\rangle\) changes a link by \(z_j\mapsto e^{i(\alpha_j-\alpha_{j+1})}z_j\). Each phase appears once with each sign. Turn up g: the individual links move while the loop factor stays put. This is the cancellation used in numerical calculations with normalized overlap links.

Why does the overlap product give the geometric phase?

For the displayed spinor at fixed β, a small azimuthal step gives a link whose phase is \(-\sin^2(\beta/2)\,d\phi\) to first order:

\[\begin{aligned}\langle u(\phi+d\phi)|u(\phi)\rangle&=1-i\sin^2(\beta/2)\,d\phi+O(d\phi^2),\\ \gamma&=-\oint\sin^2(\beta/2)\,d\phi=-\frac{\Omega}{2}\pmod{2\pi}.\end{aligned}\]

Multiplying the normalized links adds these infinitesimal phases in the continuum limit; local rephasings still cancel around the closed circuit.

What exactly is the finite-sample error?

With g = 0, every link before normalization is \(\cos^2(\beta/2)+\sin^2(\beta/2)e^{\mp2\pi i/N}\). Its normalized phase raised to the Nth power gives the sampled result. At finite N, the shorter great-circle arcs between spin directions enclose a different area from the smooth latitude. Refinement makes these paths agree. Changing g cannot repair a coarse mesh: it only changes the description.

The error shown is \(|\arg(e^{i\gamma_N}e^{-i\gamma})|\). Neighboring orthogonal states make a normalized link undefined; the calculation rejects such a link rather than assigning it zero phase. The sample counts offered here avoid that singular case for these latitudes.

For reproducibility, the phase choices are \(\alpha_j=g[\sin(2\pi j/N)+0.37\cos(4\pi j/N)]\). Angles and g are in radians. The JSON export records every link and the parameters; no random seed is needed.

What this teaches us about anyons

These loops on the sphere can all be contracted, yet changing their area changes the phase. Geometric does not mean topological. An anyon calculation must identify the appropriate low-energy states and distinguish the transport that depends on their braid from other contributions.

In a degenerate multiplet, adiabatic transport can mix states through a matrix, as in Wilczek and Zee’s construction. Such mixing alone does not establish anyons. A gap to unwanted states and a small splitting within a fusion space are different energy scales; checking transport times also requires the path and transition matrix elements. The connected notes discuss these conditions.

A phase becomes visible only against a reference: return to the interferometer. To calculate matrices in a shared anyon state space, try changing fusion bases.
